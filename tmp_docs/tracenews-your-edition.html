Your Edition

The subscriber side in the same broadsheet language. The console is a monitoring room; this is not — it is the reader's standing relationship with the Nigerian press. Their own masthead, their own dateline, one editorial sentence about their reading before any number. Scope is deliberately bigger than the five sections we have circled: we hold 867 politician pages and 142 outlet profiles and currently give a reader no way to follow any of them, no way to read what we publish, and no way to exercise their data rights without emailing us.

Design

Reading summary — the landing

The lede is indigo, not amber — the reader's gap is information, not an alarm on their desk. Consent state sits in the byline where it is always visible. Figures are unequal and rule-separated. The standing column carries alerts, what they follow, and a privacy panel that makes control feel present rather than buried in settings.

Direction

One grammar, two rooms

The console and the edition share every token, component and rule — masthead and dateline, a sentence before numbers, rules not boxes, unequal figures, mono numerals as texture, one accent per screen. What differs is register, and it should.

The Desk — staff

A monitoring room. Amber accent: things are owed and overdue. The standing column shows the platform's pulse — the wire, health, the ledger. It answers: what needs me?

Your Edition — reader

A personal edition. Indigo accent: gaps are information, not alarms — nothing here is the reader's fault or duty. The standing column shows their alerts, what they follow, and their control over their own data. It answers: what did I miss?
