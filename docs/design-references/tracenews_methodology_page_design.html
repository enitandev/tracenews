TraceNews Independence Index

How We Score Nigerian Media Independence

The TII is a 0–100 behavioural score applied to Nigerian news outlets. This page explains every signal, every threshold, and every editorial decision behind the numbers — because a media intelligence instrument you cannot examine is one you should not trust.

Last updated: June 2026 · 82 outlets scored · 30-day rolling window
01

What the TII Measures

The TraceNews Independence Index (TII) measures one specific thing: how freely a Nigerian news outlet makes editorial decisions independent of political and commercial power. It does not measure accuracy, quality of writing, or factual reliability. It measures editorial behaviour — who is sourced, what stories are chosen, what language is used, and what stories are absent.

Every outlet is scored on a 30-day rolling sample of up to 150 published stories. The sample is drawn from RSS ingestion. Scores update periodically or when significant changes in an outlet's behaviour are detected.

A higher score means greater editorial independence from power. A lower score means editorial choices that consistently defer to government, party, or commercial interests — whether by direct control, ownership alignment, or access-journalism incentives.

02

The Six Signals

The TII is a weighted average of six behavioural signals, each computed from the outlet's story sample. Weights reflect the signal's discriminating power in the Nigerian media context — source hierarchy and original reporting together account for 55% of the score because they are the most reliably measurable indicators of editorial independence.

S1 30%
Source Hierarchy

Does the outlet rely primarily on official government sources, or does it seek independent verification and alternative voices? We assess whether stories draw predominantly from government spokespeople, press releases, and official statements versus journalists, civil society, or independently verified accounts.

Example: A story on fuel scarcity that quotes only the NNPC spokesperson scores low. One that also quotes petrol station owners, independent economists, and civil society analysts scores higher.

S2 25%
Original Reporting

What proportion of stories are original reporting versus republished wire copy or press releases? We compare each story against NAN wire content and detect semantic near-duplicates — stories where the outlet added no original reporting and simply reformatted a government wire or press release.

Example: An outlet running 13 of 14 stories as near-identical NAN wire copy scores very low on S2. An outlet where most stories carry original bylines and new information scores high.

S3 20%
Omission Pattern

Does the outlet consistently avoid covering major accountability stories that the wider Nigerian media ecosystem reports? We identify clusters of stories covered by ≥60% of outlets in the ecosystem and check whether this outlet is absent from them — a pattern of strategic silence on accountability topics.

Example: If 35 outlets cover a court ruling against a government agency but a specific outlet never publishes it across multiple such cases, this depresses S3.

S4 10%
Lexical Deference

Does the outlet's language use political honorifics, PR framing, or deferential terms toward officials? We score the density of terms like "His Excellency," "magnanimous," "graciously," and promotional superlatives that signal editorial deference rather than neutral reporting.

Example: An outlet that consistently writes "His Excellency graciously approved a landmark directive" scores lower than one that writes "The governor approved the policy."

S5 10%
Story Selection

What proportion of the outlet's coverage focuses on accountability, oversight, and public-interest topics versus government announcements, ceremonies, and promotional content? We classify stories by category and assess the ratio of accountability-relevant coverage.

Example: An outlet whose politics section is dominated by "Governor commissions borehole in community" and similar announcement coverage scores lower than one where government-accountability stories predominate.

S6 5%
Editorial Indicators

Does the outlet publish corrections, engage with source disputes, or show evidence of editorial independence processes? This is the lightest-weighted signal because it is harder to systematically measure from RSS feeds, but carries meaningful signal when present or provably absent.

Example: An outlet that has published corrections and named both sides in editorial disputes scores higher than one with no public record of corrections or source engagement.

03

The Three Tiers

The weighted average of S1–S6 produces a raw TII score from 0 to 100. This score maps to one of three tiers. Tier boundaries are not arbitrary — they reflect meaningful behavioural thresholds in the Nigerian media context: below 35 indicates systematic deference to power; above 60 indicates a consistent pattern of editorial independence; between 35 and 60 is the large middle of commercial Nigerian journalism.

Mainstream 35 – 59
Watchdog 60 – 100

04

Monitoring Spirit Signals

Beyond individual outlet scores, TraceNews surfaces patterns across the entire Nigerian media ecosystem for each story. These ecosystem signals describe observable coverage facts — not editorial motives, and not assertions about any individual outlet's decision-making.

One-Sided Coverage
Coverage concentrated in one tier

Fires when a story's coverage is dominated by one editorial tier while another is largely silent. This surfaces both cases: accountability outlets covering something government-aligned outlets are not reporting, and government-aligned outlets amplifying something accountability outlets are ignoring.

Threshold: One tier ≥ 60% of coverage AND another tier ≤ 10% of coverage, with total outlet count ≥ 5 and the imbalance persisting for ≥ 2 hours.
Copy-and-Paste
Most outlets ran the same report

Fires when the majority of outlets covering a story published substantially identical content — indicating the story propagated through the ecosystem without independent verification. When the identical source is a government wire (NAN, VON), this is noted specifically: the government effectively authored the story that 12 outlets ran.

Threshold: ≥ 60% of scored covering outlets have S2 score < 40 (wire-republishing pattern), with minimum 4 scored outlets.

Both signals are direction-agnostic by design: the same logic fires whether government-aligned outlets are silent or whether watchdog outlets are silent. The instrument sees both — because a monitoring system that only points in one direction is a propaganda instrument, not an editorial one.

05

Special Cases and Structural Caps

The TII methodology applies cleanly to general-purpose Nigerian newsrooms. Several outlet types require adjustments where the standard signals would produce systematically inaccurate readings — not because the outlets are better or worse, but because the methodology's assumptions don't hold for their editorial model.

Language Service Outlets
S3 signal defaulted to neutral

The S3 omission signal compares an outlet against English-language accountability clusters. Language service outlets covering Nigerian news in Hausa or Yoruba structurally cannot match these English clusters — not because they suppress stories, but because the same events are reported in different language contexts. S3 is set to 70 (neutral) for these outlets.

Specialist / Beat Outlets
S3 signal excluded

Outlets covering a specific beat (technology, finance, pan-African analysis, sports) should not be penalised for not covering political accountability stories outside their editorial mandate. S3 is set to 70 (neutral) for confirmed specialist outlets.

Federal Government Outlets
Score capped at 30

Outlets owned and editorially directed by the federal government are structurally incapable of editorial independence on matters of government conduct, regardless of individual story quality. Their scores are capped at 30 to reflect this structural reality.

Known-Aligned Outlets
Score capped at 34 (Govt tier)

Outlets where ownership ties to political figures or parties constitute a documented structural fact — not inferred from behavioural patterns alone — are capped at 34 to reflect that structural editorial alignment, regardless of their day-to-day content scores.

06

What We Do Not Claim

The TII describes observable editorial behaviour across a 30-day sample. Several things that readers might infer from a score are things we explicitly do not assert.

  • ✕ We do not assert that any outlet accepted payment for coverage, engaged in "brown envelope" journalism, or made any financially corrupt editorial decision. The promotional_alignment_flag describes an observable behavioural pattern — anomalously positive sentiment combined with low source diversity — not a transaction.
  • ✕ We do not assert that any journalist or editor acted in bad faith. Structural constraints (ownership, access dependence, commercial pressure) can produce low TII scores without any individual making a dishonest decision.
  • ✕ We do not claim to measure the factual accuracy of individual stories. An outlet can produce factually accurate stories while still deferring systematically to official sources and narratives.
  • ✕ We do not claim that One-Sided Coverage signals represent deliberate suppression. "Government-aligned outlets have not reported this" is a fact about which outlets published stories in a window — not an assertion that any outlet chose to suppress the story.
  • ✕ We do not claim that the TII is final or infallible. It is a behavioural measurement on a 30-day rolling window. An outlet that changes its editorial practices will see its score change. We welcome methodological challenges.

Coverage snapshots are taken every 10 minutes per cluster and retained permanently, enabling historical analysis of how coverage patterns changed over time. This is the data layer that makes it possible to detect Memory Hole patterns — stories that spike and then quietly disappear from coverage before resolution.

The entity registry covers 10 categories: Legislature (450), Governors (217), Security apparatus (73), Executive (33), Party leadership (28), Judiciary (27), Power brokers (24), Civil society (15), Traditional rulers (10), and Business crossovers (5). The registry is updated as appointments, elections, and party changes occur.

Disputes, Data Access, and Contact

Challenge a score or request data

If you believe a TII score is inaccurate — whether you represent a scored outlet or are a researcher who has identified a methodological flaw — we will review the challenge and publish our response. We take methodological accountability seriously: the instrument cannot demand it of others if it does not practice it.

Researchers, NGOs, and funders may request the full scoring dataset for specific outlets or date ranges, including historical TII score records and coverage snapshot data.
